# Projects: Choosing and Switching Models

Community projects that compare, benchmark, or switch decision models, grouped by sub-type. Numbers are author-reported. An entry with no model in parentheses ran on TypeSafe Jev; every other entry names its model. The procedure, code sketch, variants, and field lessons are in `choosing-and-switching-models.md`, not in `prior-art/`.

**Benchmarks and indexes** (cross-model; per-vendor numbers are vendor-reported)
- Jev Decision Index: 132,422 frozen requests, 37 benchmarks, 30+ open models plus Jev. No prompt tuning. Errors score zero (Clef, Kev, Nimble, pplx-decider, others): [HF Space](https://huggingface.co/spaces/multimodalart/jev-decision-index), [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/jev-decision-index-ranks-30-open-weight-decision-models). Decision Index 0.2.1 for Ollama models: tev1:0.8b 12.85, tev1 29.24, nimble 39.57, Jev 1.13 57.91 (Tev1, Nimble): [llmconfigurator.com](https://llmconfigurator.com/en/guides/decision-models)
- BenchLM decision-models board: 112 systems scored on Intelligence, Calibration, Speed, and Cost. Reported calibration: Jev 88.03, CLM-8B 48.48 (many): [benchlm.ai](https://benchlm.ai/decision-models)
- JevBench (Benchmark Heaven): 32 models, 308 sealed items. Reported: decider-4b v2 64.1, Jev 63.3. r=0.86 with the Decision Index (many): [madebyagents.com](https://www.madebyagents.com/benchmarks/jevbench). Leaderboard discussion: [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49849014)
- AIM System One Index and AIM-Decision. Reported index: Kev-27b 80, Jev 1.13 66, Clef 52, Clef Flash 30. Some small models gave degenerate "done" or "wait" answers (Kev, Clef, CLM, others): [aimultiple.com/system-one](https://aimultiple.com/system-one), [aimultiple.com/decision-models](https://aimultiple.com/decision-models)
- S1MB, 23 models, 137 tasks, 14,009 cases. Jev 1.13 Borda 86.51, bekko-400m 76.21; bekko falls to 54.48 on novel tasks (Kev, Laya, Tev1, decider, others): [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/hotchpotch/system-one-mosaic-benchmark)
- sysone-bench, sealed byte-identical inputs and no per-model prompt tuning. Jev 0.9065, kev-4b 0.8556, Laya 0.6863 (Kev, Laya, Tev1): [instax-dutta/sysone-bench](https://github.com/instax-dutta/sysone-bench)
- amplifying.ai, an index of 38 models and a catalog of nine benchmark suites, including S1MB (all models): [amplifying.ai](https://amplifying.ai/decision-models)
- Cross-model ECE roundup. Reported: Jev 0.074, Nimble v2 0.024, pplx-decider 0.018 (many): [laurencemoroney.com](https://laurencemoroney.com/2026/10/02/decision-models-explained.html)
- Clone benchmark-disagreement review. Jev's Banking77 ranges from 76.3% to 87.0% across evals; Laya drops from 0.950 at 4 labels to 0.425 at 77 (Laya, SemIf, Nimble, Kev): [creativeainews.com](https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/open-jev-clones-benchmark-disagreement-2026/)

**Vendor-reported launch numbers**
- Perplexity 11-test panel, 7,210 rows: pplx-decider 85.71% vs Jev 84.51%, RAGTruth 88.8 vs 77.3. No prompts, item IDs, or calibration published (pplx-decider-v1-27b): [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/perplexity-open-sources-27b-decider-edges-jev-on-11-test-panel), [benchlm panel](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/perplexity-decision-panel), [benchlm RAGTruth panel](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/ragtruth-perplexity-panel)
- Clef launch evals: BANKING77 94.2 vs Jev 79.7. Clef wins 3 of 4 TypeSafe workflow evals but loses agent-trace observability (68.5 vs 71.6) (Clef, Clef-flash): [blog.cloudflare.com](https://blog.cloudflare.com/clef-decision-models/)
- Ollama 0.35 decision lane on 13 datasets: Jev 76.0, Nimble 75.7, Tev1-4B 73.3, Tev1-0.8B 63.5. Jev leads on reasoning (Nimble, Tev1 via Ollama): [sotaaz.com](https://sotaaz.com/post/ollama-decision-models-en), [kmail.at](https://kmail.at/blog/system-one-goes-local), [jevainews](https://jevainews.com/news/bespoke-nimble/)
- Nimble public benchmarks, 3,880 human-labelled records: Jev 76.0% vs Nimble 74.8%, Jev lower ECE on 11 of 13 subsets, Nimble ahead on Score tasks (Nimble): [PUBLIC_BENCHMARKS.md](https://github.com/bespokelabsai/nimble/blob/main/docs/PUBLIC_BENCHMARKS.md)
- Kev README: familiar vs novel sources, automation share at a 5% error budget per checkpoint, fitted temperatures. A temperature fit cuts Kev-9B confident errors from 8.2% to 2.4% (Jev 3.7%) (Kev): [jaredpalmer/kev](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev)
- Strands Decider 2B, open weights with training data and scripts: 3rd of 33 in the 2B class on JevBench, tracks Brier alongside accuracy, about 115 ms on an RTX 3090 (Strands Decider 2B): [strandsagents.com](https://strandsagents.com/blog/introducing-strands-decider/)
- jiwo-4b: Decision Index 44.97, 1st of 3–6B models on the live board (jiwo): [jiwidi/jiwo](https://github.com/jiwidi/jiwo). Liquid d1: matches or beats GPT-6.1 Sol on 4 of 6 apps at 19–200× less cost (Liquid d1): [liquid.ai](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/d1-decision-model)

**Head-to-head comparisons** (independent)
- Opper side-by-side on 362 fresh questions: Jev 96.5%, Kev 4B 95.7%. Kev's calibration drifted more on Stack Exchange and GitHub tasks. Jev adds about 257 hidden input tokens per request (Kev): [opper.ai](https://opper.ai/blog/jev-vs-kev-open-decision-model)
- Jev vs Kev: Kev-9B leads support routing (0.952 vs 0.897) but trails on MMLU-Pro (0.515 vs 0.840) (Kev): [orcarouter.ai](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/jev-vs-kev). Laya vs Kev: [orcarouter.ai](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/laya-vs-kev)
- hard-decisions, 3,600 ProofWriter problems by proof depth, all requests kept verbatim: Jev 83.8%, GLiDE 82.9% (76.4% on rerun), GPT-6 Luna 64.1%, Kev-9B 58.6%, Laya 42.4% (GPT-6 Luna, GLiDE, Kev, Laya): [hard-decisions.anth.us](https://hard-decisions.anth.us/)
- Anthus OpenAI Decisions preview test: at ≥99% stated confidence Luna was right 68% of the time vs 98.9% for Jev; 78% of Luna's errors were stated at ≥95%, and AUROC fell to 0.51 at five chained steps (OpenAI Decisions API): [anth.us](https://anth.us/blog/openai-decisions-api-preview/)
- Stress test with 2–128 candidates, reordered options, and harder distractors. At 128 options: Jev 60%, Laya 39%. Laya's ECE rose from 0.032 to 0.573, and reordering changed half its answers (Laya): [gazelle93/decision-models-under-pressure](https://github.com/gazelle93/decision-models-under-pressure)
- Shell-command approval at a 0.9 threshold. Jev and Liquid d1 approved no unsafe commands and 91–92% of safe ones. Kev 4B had no false-safe approvals but approved only 63% of safe ones. Tev1 4B had 3 false-safe approvals, Solar Decide 1, Mercury Decide 12. Kev scores bunch near 0.9 (Liquid d1, Kev, Tev1, Mercury Decide, Solar Decide): [dbohdan/strument experiment](https://github.com/dbohdan/strument/tree/c2320ca142f77495ecdcadd3cd7a4c9b9445369f/doc/experiments/2026-10-decision-models)
- Red Hat benchmark of decision models vs trained guardrail classifiers vs LLM judges. Prompt injection: Qwen3.6-35B 89.3%, DeBERTa 89.0%, DiffusionGemma-26B 87.72%, Jev 86.35%, Laya 85.44%. Laya content safety 57.87% → 75.20% with tuned definitions. Decision models did not reliably beat either alternative (Laya, DiffusionGemma via vLLM /v1/systemone): [developers.redhat.com](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2026/10/02/benchmarking-ai-decision-models-against-traditional-guardrails)
- Decision models vs trained classifiers vs LLMs for decision gates, 8 checkpoints; a this-that-model → Jev cascade matches Jev on CLINC-150 at 0.43 of the cost (Laya, Kev, decider-2b, this-that-model, Nimble): [arXiv:2610.00346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00346)
- Independent harnesses. nibzard has 5 suites, including option-order sensitivity and forced choice with no right answer: [nibzard/decision-model-benchmark](https://github.com/nibzard/decision-model-benchmark). ickma2311 is pre-registered with kill/go criteria; a bge-small + logistic-regression encoder hit 93.3% on Banking77, above Jev: [ickma2311/jev-baselines-eval](https://github.com/ickma2311/jev-baselines-eval)
- Ollama vs Ollaya serving of the same weights: Nimble ECE 0.122 raw vs 0.022 with a fitted temperature (Nimble): [pyshine.com](https://pyshine.com/Ollaya-Run-Open-Decision-Models-Locally/)

**Conformance and compatibility**
- jevcompat, a 48-requirement conformance suite for any `/v1/systemone` server. Of 8 open servers, 2 passed every required check; 4 capped Choice at 26–128 options; `confidence` meant three different things. Run it before swapping backends (Kev, Laya, decider, Von, others): [mandu5/jevcompat](https://github.com/mandu5/jevcompat)
- Pydantic AI `SystemOneModel`, one client for any `/v1/systemone` backend, with per-backend `max_choice_options` and `max_score_levels` and its own `confidence` statistic (Jev, CLM, Laya, Ollama): [pydantic.dev](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/models/system-one/)
- Ollama `/v1/systemone` reference: entropy-based `confidence`, option limit "depends on the model" (Nimble, Tev1, Clef via Ollama): [docs.ollama.com](https://docs.ollama.com/api/systemone). Confidence and thresholds guide: [llmconfigurator.com](https://llmconfigurator.com/en/guides/decision-models/confidence-and-thresholds)
- Ollaya, a local runtime that applies each model's fitted temperature at load (Nimble, Laya): [ollaya-dev/ollaya](https://github.com/ollaya-dev/ollaya)

**Gateways, adapters, and routers that switch backends** (more in `prior-art/projects/embedding-in-infrastructure.md`)
- OpenRouter `/api/v1/systemone`: Jev through typesafe-sdk with a `base_url` swap; `client.models.list()` fails there (Jev; Clef and decider listed): [openrouter.ai](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/community/typesafe-sdk.md)
- Vercel AI Gateway decision fallbacks: rerun an uncertain answer on another model with `when: {question, confidenceBelow}`. A language-model fallback returns `confidence: 0` and empty `probabilities` (Jev, Laya, Liquid d1, language models): [vercel.com](https://vercel.com/docs/ai-gateway/modalities/decision.md)
- jev-switch, a Rust DAG router with failover across Vercel, TypeSafe, OpenRouter and local Laya (Jev, Laya): [ARCJ137442/jev-switch](https://github.com/ARCJ137442/jev-switch)
- BrighTO-Router, a Rust LLM gateway exposing `/v1/systemone` and `/v1/decisions` as separate adapter surfaces; reported ~2 ms p50 overhead (Laya, Ollaya, Jev-compatible): [thusinh1969/BrighTO_Router](https://github.com/thusinh1969/BrighTO_Router)
- decidealot, one Docker HTTP + MCP service in front of several models (Laya, Von, CLM, Jev proxy): [psyb0t/decidealot](https://hub.docker.com/r/psyb0t/decidealot)
- stuntd, a learning proxy that trains a head per decision site, shadows, then serves locally with provider fallback; reported 71–99% local coverage (Laya, Jev): [bladedevoff/stuntd](https://github.com/bladedevoff/stuntd)
- Tiltmeter, a `/v1/systemone` proxy that alerts without labels on version switches, drift, and answers crowding a threshold: [abe75ch/tiltmeter](https://github.com/abe75ch/tiltmeter). jev-trust, middleware that measures calibration from recorded outcomes: [pypi.org/project/jev-trust](https://pypi.org/project/jev-trust/)
- pi-verdict, one gate that runs on TypeSafe, OpenRouter, Workers AI, or Vercel AI Gateway (Jev): [jesset/pi-verdict](https://github.com/jesset/pi-verdict)

**Migration and porting write-ups**
- Ollama base-URL swap: `TYPESAFE_BASE_URL=http://localhost:11434` and the TypeSafe SDK runs unchanged (Nimble, Tev1 via Ollama): [ollama.com/blog](https://ollama.com/blog/ollama-now-supports-jev-style-decision-models)
- Calling Clef with the TypeSafe JS SDK through a custom `fetch` that rewrites the path and unwraps `result` (Clef, Clef-flash): [flaviocopes.com](https://flaviocopes.com/clef/)
- OpenAI Decisions vs Jev, with advice to keep each behind a thin adapter (gpt-6-luna): [firecrawl.dev](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/openai-decisions-api-vs-jev)
- clef-rag, which ships Jev-tuned thresholds and says to re-tune them on Clef (Clef, Clef-flash): [MersivMedia/clef-rag](https://github.com/MersivMedia/clef-rag)
- Janus, which measures on your data whether a decision model beats the fallback before routing; its best threshold moved from 0.67 to 0.37 between datasets: [FirasSX914/Janus](https://github.com/FirasSX914/Janus)
